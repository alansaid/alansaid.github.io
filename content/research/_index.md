I study how people interact with information through recommender systems and personalization. My research connects reliable experimental evidence with people's experiences and the environmental and societal consequences of personalized systems.

{{< research-nav >}}

## Evaluation and reproducibility

*What does an evaluation result allow us to conclude?*

Evaluation depends on datasets, sampling, tuning and software as well as algorithms. I study how these choices shape comparisons and what evidence supports a reported improvement. This work spans reproducible benchmarking with [RiVal]({{< relref "/publications/2014-said-rival" >}}), audits of experimental frameworks, and methods for interpreting partial or conflicting reproduction results.

## People and personalization

*What do people need from personalized systems?*

I study how preferences change over time and how people understand and trust personalized systems. Through studies of music listening, explanations and conversational agents, I examine when personalization helps, how context shapes its value, and how trust develops through use.

## Sustainability and responsibility

*What should count as a cost or benefit of personalization?*

I examine the energy use and carbon footprint of recommender-system research and contribute measurement tools such as [EMERS]({{< relref "/publications/2024-wegmeth-emers" >}}). I also study wider consequences: how personalization can encourage consumption, whose interests systems serve, and how people understand fairness criteria. These questions connect environmental measurement with system design and evaluation.
